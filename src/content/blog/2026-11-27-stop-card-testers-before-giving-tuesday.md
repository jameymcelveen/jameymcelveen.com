Giving Tuesday is next week. If you run an online giving page, here is something worth checking before then: card testers.

Card testing is when fraudsters take a list of stolen or made-up card numbers and run them through a website one after another to see which ones work. Donation pages are a favorite target. They take small amounts, they do not ship anything, and they are built to make giving easy.

The damage is real even when no money moves. Every attempt can cost the organization a fee, a flood of declines can put its payment account at risk, and the cards that work get used somewhere else.

## A few things that help

1. **Watch the pattern, not the single charge.** Lots of small attempts, lots of declines, and many cards from one source in a short time are the tell.
2. **Slow them down at the front door.** Rate limits at the gateway or API cut off the rapid-fire runs without bothering real donors.
3. **Add friction only when something looks off.** A real donor should never notice. A bot should hit a wall.
4. **Require the full card details,** like the security code and ZIP, so guessed numbers fail sooner.
5. **Turn on your payment processor's fraud tools.** Most have them, and many sites never switch them on.
6. **Alert a human.** When the pattern shows up, someone should know right away, not on Monday.

Giving days and Sunday mornings are exactly when these attacks like to hide in the crowd.
